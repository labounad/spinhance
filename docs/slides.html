Spinhance
Reading molecular structure out of a cheap, blurry NMR spectrum.

A 90 MHz benchtop spectrum looks uninterpretable — but physics says it encodes the same chemical shifts and couplings as a $1M magnet. We train a model to recover those parameters, fully characterizing a molecule's spin system from low-field data.

Motivation · chemical biology

Drug discovery runs on synthesis — and synthesis runs on mechanistic insight.

  • A benchtop NMR (90 MHz) couples directly to a reactor: in situ, non-destructive, real-time tracking as the reaction progresses.

The catch & the reframe

The blur is scrambled information — not lost information.

As field drops, peak separation Δν shrinks toward the coupling J; multiplets overlap and go second-order. But physics says the same δ and J are still encoded — drag 90→600 MHz and watch the same molecule resolve.

Stage 2 · estimate shifts & couplings

The model needs plausible spin systems — not measured ones.

Key hypothesis: to learn the inverse map, the model only needs spin systems that look like they came from a real molecule — physically and chemically plausible — not literature-accurate NMR.

So we estimate δ and J with fast heuristics instead of measuring or computing them.

  • δ and J (²J, ³J, ⁴J) by substructure matching against reference tables.
  • Sourced from nmrdb2 and Pretsch et al. (Tables of Spectral Data).
  • Karplus equation for ³J (dihedral-angle dependence).
  • Small Gaussian noise regularizes the values — variety, not memorized constants.
Payoff: a near-free generator — millions of realistic spin systems, no quantum chemistry per molecule.
Yiming. The bet: plausible > accurate for training the inverse map. Heuristics from nmrdb2 + Pretsch tables; Karplus for 3J; Gaussian jitter for diversity.
Stage 3 · pyspin — the physics engine

Spin systems → exact spectra, from first principles.

H  =  Σi σi Iz,i  +  Σi<j Jij Ii·Ij
Zeeman — field & shifts σicoupling — spin–spin Jij
  • NMR records energy transitions between nuclear spin states; the energies are the eigenvalues of this spin Hamiltonian.
  • Selection rules pick the allowed gaps → a stick spectrum, convolved with a fixed-width Lorentzian to mimic the spectrometer.
  • N spin-½ nuclei → a 2N-dimensional space — exact diagonalization is intractable.
  • pyspin uses spin-cluster decomposition + local approximate bases → linear scaling, near-exact accuracy.
1500 / sec
spin systems → spectra (license-free, open source)
Lucas. The Hamiltonian has a field-dependent Zeeman term (shifts) + a coupling term (J). Eigenvalues = state energies; transitions → sticks → Lorentzian broadening = the spectrum. 2^N is intractable; clustering + local bases give linear scaling at ~1500 spectra/s.

Model · the structured decoder — the leap

The spin system is a graph — so predict a graph.

SPECTRUM

16,384 pts

conv stem → ppm-tagged feature tokens

▶
ENCODER

self-attention

tokens attend across the whole spectrum — links coupled multiplets

▶
8 QUERIES → DECODER

cross-attention

one query per spin group gathers its evidence (set prediction)

▶
HEADS

nodes + edges

δ & degeneracy per group; symmetric J & presence per pair

Each spin-group query learns to look at the regions of the spectrum that belong to its group; a symmetric edge head predicts all 28 couplings — so the unordered, graph-structured answer is built in. (We also tested support-region tokenization — feeding detected peak regions as tokens — but global attention already captured it, so we dropped it.)

Sam. The key idea: an unordered graph (8 nodes, 28 edges) deserves a structured decoder, not a flat regressor. Encoder = self-attention over conv tokens (ppm-positioned). Decoder = 8 learned spin-group QUERIES cross-attend the spectrum (DETR-style set prediction). Heads = node (δ, n) + symmetric edge (J, presence). Result: shift 7.5× better (0.037 ppm), J 0.59 Hz, F1 0.94, deg 0.95. Support-region tokens were tested but added nothing — dropped.
Model · two refinements & the honest test

Polishing the last failure modes — and checking we didn't memorize.

  • Peak-channel input: annotate the spectrum with peak-pick information to sharpen shift localization.
  • Soft-equivalence flag: a learnable edge label marking two groups that share a shift but differ in coupling. When flagged, their shifts are averaged — so an accidentally-degenerate pair renders as one peak, not a spurious doublet.
  • The flag solved the visual artifact; shift MAE matched its predecessor. Both are our production models.
No overfitting: on the never-seen held-out test split, accuracy was identical to validation.

Scaling & roadmap · where this goes

From 64k ChEMBL to 3M+ PubChem — and beyond fixed N.

Now
  • Every model above was trained & compared on the 64k ChEMBL 8-group set.
  • The data pipeline is now scaled to 3.1M PubChem 8-group systems → spectra.
  • Scaled-up production models (more data → larger networks) are training on it now.
The long game
  • Train a family of fixed-N spin-group models (N = 2, 3, … groups) on PubChem.
  • Train a classifier that reads the number of spin groups directly from a spectrum.
  • Compose them → spectrum → spin system for arbitrary small molecules, not just 8-group ones.
The endgame: any small-molecule ¹H spectrum, from any field — decoded into a complete structural answer.
Lifting the fixed-N restriction is the path to generality: a classifier picks N, the matching fixed-N model recovers the spin system. Pipeline already produces 3.1M PubChem systems; scaled production models are training now.
Conclusion

A "low-information" benchtop spectrum → a complete structural answer.

  • What we built: a license-free, linear-scaling spin simulator (pyspin, 1500/s), a 3M-molecule data pipeline, and a graph-structured decoder that recovers shifts to 0.037 ppm and couplings to 0.59 Hz.
  • What we learned: low field genuinely encodes the full spin system; predicting it as a graph (not 44 flat numbers) was the leap; and plausible training data beats expensive measured data.
  • Why it matters: high-field-quality structure from a ~$50k benchtop — enabling cheaper, in-situ reaction monitoring that accelerates synthesis and small-molecule drug discovery.
